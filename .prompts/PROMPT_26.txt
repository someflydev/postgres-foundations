## Task

### 1. Capstone 3: Event-heavy operations

Layout identical to capstones 01/02 under
`capstones/03-event-heavy-ops/`.

- Scenario: an operational analytics platform for an IoT-adjacent
  product. 2M events/day from ~40k devices; dashboards for support
  engineers; hot queries are "last 24 hours by device" and
  "anomaly backtrace for a specific device over 7 days"; retention
  18 months with cold-archive after 90 days.
- Constraints:
  - Postgres 16; **no TimescaleDB** (the learner will argue under
    what signals Timescale becomes the right next step — this is
    the capstone's explicit decision-engine-style question).
  - Partitioning by range on event_time is required.
  - BRIN on event_time + a B-tree on device_id per partition is
    the starting point; justify any deviation.
  - Logical replication to a read replica for analytics is
    considered; writeup must state whether to do it now and why.
  - pg_stat_statements is required; ties in to admin track.
- Deliverables: schema + partitioning strategy, retention script,
  indexes, critical queries including a "top-N devices by recent
  anomaly count" query and a "device timeline" query, a writeup
  that includes "when would TimescaleDB be worth it?" as its
  own section.
- Rubric emphasizes operational posture + extension justification.
- Reference solution:
  - Declaratively partitioned events table (monthly).
  - BRIN + B-tree indexing plan.
  - `detach_partition` + export-to-cold retention script.
  - A short runbook for "a query is slow — where to start".
  - A writeup including the explicit "TimescaleDB later" criteria.

### 2. Capstone 4: Modernization bridge

Layout under `capstones/04-modernization-bridge/`.

- Scenario: a company with a legacy monolith database containing
  `customers`, `orders`, and `products` in a schema that predates
  any sane modeling discipline. A new product team wants to build
  a fresh service that needs access to subsets of that data. The
  organization does not have buy-in yet to migrate the monolith.
  The team must ship the new service with accurate reads against
  the legacy data, write-locally with strict correctness, and
  plan an eventual migration without prematurely shipping a
  rewrite.
- Constraints:
  - Postgres 16 on both sides (the legacy DB and the new service
    DB). In the lab, this is the pg + pg-replica pair configured
    as independent databases (not truly replicating), with
    `postgres_fdw` wiring the "new" database to see the "legacy"
    tables as foreign.
  - Use `postgres_fdw`, `IMPORT FOREIGN SCHEMA`, and at least one
    materialized view on the new side to cache an aggregate from
    the legacy side.
  - Discuss but do not implement logical replication for an
    incremental migration; writeup must articulate when that step
    becomes the right next move.
  - RLS on the new side for tenant isolation even though the
    legacy side has none.
  - No Citus; capstone writeup must justify why not.
- Deliverables: a clean new-service schema; the FDW wiring; the
  chosen materialized views with refresh policy; 8+ critical
  queries (several crossing the FDW); an operational writeup
  covering failure modes of FDW and the explicit "promote to
  logical replication when X" criteria.

### 3. Cross-capstone rubric themes

Both capstones emphasize:

- "Extension selection is explainable." Require the learner to
  produce a short "extension posture" section in every writeup:
  which extensions are enabled now, which are explicitly deferred,
  and under what signals they would be adopted.
- "Operational tolerance is named." The writeup states what the
  team can and cannot operate, and shapes decisions accordingly.

Capture these themes in a shared rubric dimension by updating
`rubrics/default/` with a new `extension-posture.rubric.json` and
an updated `operational-reasoning.rubric.json`. Reference both from
capstones 3 and 4 (and backport to 1 and 2 — edit those capstones'
rubrics to include these dimensions with a small weight).
